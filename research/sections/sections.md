# Website Sections

Broad page sections for portfolios, landing pages, and general websites. Dashboards and application interfaces are out of scope.

## What counts as a section

A section is a substantial page region with one clear content purpose. Small blocks, controls, layouts, effects, and case-study subchapters are not separate section types.

Do not catalog these as sections:

- Navigation bars
- Buttons, cards, forms, tabs, accordions, tables, or modals
- Split layouts, bento grids, card grids, masonry, or centered layouts
- Carousels, marquees, sticky effects, reveals, or parallax
- Loading, empty, error, success, or other interface states
- Individual details such as role, deliverables, limitations, research, or specifications; keep them inside the relevant broad section

Every section is optional. Choose only what fits the brief and adds useful information, proof, orientation, or action.

| # | Section | Common contents |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Hero | The subject or offer, core value, useful supporting media, and main next action. |
| 2 | Introduction or Overview | Concise context for a company, person, product, service, project, page, or topic. |
| 3 | Problem and Context | The audience's actual problem, current situation, stakes, constraints, or opportunity before presenting the solution. |
| 4 | About and Story | Who the person or organization is, origin, mission, values, direction, governance, sustainability commitments, and relevant background. |
| 5 | Team and Careers | Team members, leadership, roles, expertise, culture, open positions, career paths, benefits, and hiring process. |
| 6 | Services, Features, and Capabilities | What the person, organization, product, or service offers, the useful outcomes, intended audience, scope, and relevant limits. |
| 7 | How It Works | Process steps, use-case examples, real workflow or demo, supported integrations, compatibility, responsibilities, timing, and expected result. |
| 8 | Selected Work or Archive | A curated or browsable collection of projects, case studies, experiments, side projects, or other representative work. |
| 9 | Project or Case Study | Project context, challenge, role, process, decisions, solution, implementation, outcome, and lessons as relevant. |
| 10 | Gallery or Showreel | Images, motion, audio, video, artifacts, captions, and credits that let the work speak visually. |
| 11 | Clients and Partners | Real clients, customers, collaborators, partners, sponsors, or integrations with the exact relationship. |
| 12 | Trust and Recognition | Attributed testimonials or reviews, security or compliance assurance, awards, press coverage, qualifications, and certifications. |
| 13 | Results, Statistics, and Impact | Verified outcomes, before-and-after evidence, neutral statistics, research findings, or social impact with context and source. |
| 14 | Comparison and Alternatives | Meaningful differences between products, services, plans, approaches, competitors, or the status quo. |
| 15 | Pricing and Plans | Prices, packages, inclusions, important limits, billing terms, and the relevant action. |
| 16 | Content, Resources, and Events | Articles, reports, documentation, news, guides, downloads, events, programs, schedules, speakers, or participation details. |
| 17 | FAQ | Real recurring questions with direct answers and links to deeper information when needed. |
| 18 | Contact and Inquiry | Contact routes, locations, opening hours, project or sales inquiry, quote request, relevant expectations, and response timing. |
| 19 | Signup, Registration, or Application | Newsletter, waitlist, booking, event registration, membership, application, or donation task with necessary context. |
| 20 | Call to Action | One clear decision after enough context, with a specific reason and next action. |
| 21 | Footer | Secondary navigation, contact details, legal links, social links, newsletter signup, location or language controls, and copyright as relevant. |
