Two hundred years of one family

  1. Chōjirō

    Black Raku named Amadera, the Nunnery, made in Kyoto in the late 1500s.

  2. Ichinyū

    Kanoko Madara, Fawn Speckles: red glaze spotted with black, late 1600s.

  3. Chōnyū

    A black Raku bowl from the seventh generation, the middle of the 1700s.

  4. Tokunyū

    Red Raku, under eight centimetres tall, from the eighth generation.

  5. Ryōnyū

    Brown glaze and a design of Mount Fuji, around 1800.

How an evening runs

  • Seven o'clock

    We look at the night's bowl in photographs, then hold a copy of it.

  • Half past seven

    Tea is whisked and drunk from the copy, in silence, one guest at a time.

  • Eight o'clock

    An hour on how it was made: the clay, the small kiln, the tongs and the quench.

Eight seats a night, five Tuesdays from 13 January. Bring nothing; the copies stay at the school.
