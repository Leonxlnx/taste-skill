## 1. Core model

### Separate semantics from composition

A section name describes its job, not its geometry. About, Features, How It Works, Showcase, and Social Proof can sometimes use the same compositional family because each may present a relationship between explanation, evidence, and media. Hero, Navigation, and Footer have more specialized structural duties.

Reuse a strong family when it fits. Do not force every section into a visually unique diagram. Also do not repeat one family down the entire page without changing its rhythm or information behavior.

### Abstract references instead of copying them

Extract these properties from a reference:

- primary and secondary hierarchy;
- number and role of content zones;
- alignment anchors and negative-space ownership;
- media-to-copy relationship;
- sequence and scanning path;
- density, overlap, crop, and scale behavior;
- interaction and responsive transformation.

Do not preserve exact coordinates, brand marks, imagery, copy, colors, font choices, or signature ornamental details. Rebuild the logic for the current content and brand.

### Choose by content relationship

Use the section's actual job as the first filter:

- **Explain:** focused stack, anchored split, editorial offset.
- **Demonstrate:** stage, split demonstration, sequence rail, interactive showcase.
- **Compare:** paired field, matrix, criteria-led comparison.
- **Browse:** collection, index, shelf, filtered gallery.
- **Prove:** evidence stream, case spotlight, metric narrative.
- **Convert:** focused action, contextual action, form split.
- **Orient:** inline navigation, mega navigation, directory footer.
- **Recover:** focused 404, visual detour, guided recovery.

### Map broad website sections to the visual guide

The section guide uses broad inspiration-source labels without narrowing the site's information architecture. Map portfolio, landing-page, and general-site sections as follows:

| Semantic section | Start with these guide entries or families |
| --- | --- |
| Hero | Hero |
| Introduction or overview | About, Hero, focused stack, editorial offset |
| Problem and context | About, Features, How It Works, editorial offset |
| About and story | About |
| Team and careers | About, collection, evidence stream |
| Services, features, and capabilities | Features |
| How It Works | How It Works |
| Selected work or archive | Showcase, collection |
| Project or case study | Showcase, About, How It Works, Stats |
| Gallery or showreel | Showcase, content field, collection, full-bleed stage |
| Clients and partners | Social Proof, evidence stream, collection |
| Trust and recognition | Social Proof, evidence stream |
| Results, statistics, and impact | Stats, Social Proof |
| Comparison and alternatives | Comparison |
| Pricing and plans | Pricing, Comparison |
| Content, resources, and events | Blog, Downloads, collection |
| FAQ | FAQ |
| Contact and inquiry | Contact |
| Signup, registration, or application | Newsletter, Contact, focused stack |
| Call to action | CTA |
| Footer | Footer |

Use Store for commerce and 404 for recovery when the site needs them. Treat Navigation as page infrastructure rather than proof that a page needs another content section.

## 2. Reusable layout families

### 2.1 Full-bleed stage

One visual field owns the section while copy and actions occupy a deliberate safe zone.

Use for immersive hero, campaign CTA, visual About, download launch, showcase, or a highly visual footer. Require sufficient contrast and a purposeful crop. Avoid placing every element in the center by default.

Vary through edge anchoring, copy scale, foreground depth, crop, quiet versus active media, and whether actions sit inside or immediately after the field.

### 2.2 Anchored split

Two unequal zones connect explanation with a visual, form, product state, or proof object. The balance need not be 50/50.

Use for Hero, About, Features, Contact, Downloads, CTA, Showcase, and selected Store moments. Let one side clearly lead. Avoid alternating identical left-right splits section after section.

Vary through asymmetric ratios, vertical offset, sticky media, nested supporting objects, edge-to-edge media, and reading-order reversal when content justifies it.

### 2.3 Focused stack

One narrow reading path contains the core message, supporting copy, action, or form. Negative space and typography carry the hierarchy.

Use for 404, Newsletter, Contact, Downloads, FAQ introduction, pricing decision, or a restrained CTA. It is not automatically centered; the stack may align to a grid edge.

Vary through width, anchor, vertical position, background context, action arrangement, and whether a small functional visual joins the stack.

### 2.4 Content field

A stable content core is surrounded, framed, or interrupted by several media or evidence objects. The field may feel editorial, spatial, or systematic.

Use for About, Features, Showcase, Social Proof, Store, or an expressive Hero. Every surrounding object needs a content role or a clear compositional reason. Avoid random floating cards.

Vary through object scale, density gradient, overlap, edge cropping, ordered versus loose placement, and one dominant object among supporting ones.

### 2.5 Editorial offset

Headline, explanation, and media occupy different grid anchors and create a directional reading path rather than a symmetrical block.

Use for About, Blog, Features, Showcase, portfolio work, and brand-led storytelling. Optical alignment matters more than mathematically equal gaps.

Vary through column span, baseline relationships, oversized type, image cadence, captions, whitespace, and controlled asymmetry.

### 2.6 Collection

Repeated items form a deliberate list, grid, masonry field, horizontal shelf, or featured-plus-index composition.

Use for Blog, Downloads, Showcase, Store, Social Proof, Features, and selected FAQ indexes. Choose the collection form from item variance and browsing behavior, not from a default three-card grid.

Vary through one featured item, image ratio, grouping, density, metadata, filters, alternating spans, and list-to-grid transitions. Preserve predictable tab and reading order.

### 2.7 Sequence rail

Steps, states, or chapters share one visual path. The sequence can be vertical, horizontal, sticky, or media-led.

Use for How It Works, onboarding explanation, product demonstration, case-study narrative, or a process inside Features. Use it only when order is meaningful.

Vary through progressive media, chapter scale, alternating emphasis, sticky context, compact numbered list, or one continuous diagram. Do not turn ordinary feature copy into numbered cards.

### 2.8 Paired field

Two options, states, approaches, or products receive a common frame and comparable hierarchy.

Use for Comparison, Pricing, before-and-after explanation, or two-path CTA. State comparison criteria honestly and keep both sides readable.

Vary through side-by-side panels, a shared central axis, toggle-controlled states, or a criteria list that reveals the meaningful differences.

### 2.9 Matrix

Rows and columns expose several criteria or offerings at once.

Use for real plan comparison, specifications, compatibility, or multi-option evaluation. Do not use a matrix when only two short statements need comparison.

Vary through a sticky axis, grouped criteria, expandable details, mobile card-by-criterion transformation, or horizontal scrolling contained inside the matrix.

### 2.10 Evidence stream

Proof appears as a connected narrative: work, quote, result, source, or metric. One evidence type may lead while others support.

Use for Social Proof, Stats, Showcase, About, or a case-study close. Never invent customers, quotes, logos, metrics, awards, or outcomes.

Vary through a case spotlight, source-linked quotes, annotated metrics, client index, outcome timeline, or a media-led story. Avoid an automatic logo wall.

### 2.11 Contextual action

The CTA grows directly from the preceding argument and may share its visual or evidence context.

Use for conversion moments, Downloads, Newsletter, Contact, Store, and post-case-study action. Prefer one clear intent; secondary actions must be genuinely different.

Vary through inline action, form, split action and preview, sticky local action, or an action embedded in a product demonstration.

### 2.12 Utility directory

Navigation, legal, contact, product, and account destinations form a legible orientation system.

Use for Navigation and Footer. Structure by visitor task and information architecture. Decorative scale is secondary to wayfinding.

Vary through compact inline links, grouped columns, a large destination index, featured destinations, local subnavigation, and context-aware utility areas.

## 3. Section guide

These are candidate grammars, not required counts or templates.

### 3.1 404

- **Focused recovery:** clear missing-page message, primary path back, optional search or relevant destination.
- **Visual detour:** one meaningful visual field with a compact recovery control.
- **Guided recovery:** a small destination index when the site has several plausible next steps.
- **Contextual 404:** preserve the surrounding site shell and explain what may have moved.

Never make the joke or artwork obscure the way out.

### 3.2 About

- Editorial offset with manifesto, origin, or operating principles.
- Anchored split connecting a concise narrative with people, place, process, or artifact.
- Content field for a visual culture or studio story.
- Evidence stream when credibility is better shown through work and decisions than biography.
- Full-bleed chapter for one strong brand or place image.
- Timeline only when chronology genuinely explains the organization.

### 3.3 Blog

- Featured story plus compact index.
- Editorial list with changing image ratios and useful metadata.
- Structured grid for consistent article types.
- Topic-led index with filters or grouped collections.

Do not make every article look equally important. Preserve scanning and clear link targets.

### 3.4 CTA

- Contextual action attached to the argument it concludes.
- Focused stack for one message and one action.
- Split action with a relevant preview, artifact, or product state.
- Full-bleed stage when the campaign visual already carries meaning.

Avoid repeating the hero in smaller form or adding a second action with the same intent.

### 3.5 Comparison

- Paired field for two options or states.
- Criteria-led matrix for several offerings.
- Narrative comparison that explains trade-offs before showing details.

Keep criteria checkable, labeling neutral, and mobile reading order unambiguous.

### 3.6 Contact

- Form split with context, response expectations, and alternate channels.
- Focused form stack for a single-purpose contact path.
- Location or people-led split when physical place or responsible team matters.
- Contact directory when different requests require different owners.

State whether a prototype form works. Do not show unconditional success.

### 3.7 Downloads

- Focused launch stack with platform choices.
- Product or artifact preview beside download controls.
- Full-bleed campaign field with clear device or format actions.
- Version and platform collection when several real files exist.

Show compatibility, file type, or version only when known.

### 3.8 FAQ

- Split introduction and accordion/list.
- Focused question index with progressive disclosure.
- Topic-grouped FAQ for several distinct concern areas.
- Contextual FAQ placed near the decision it supports instead of one long catch-all section.

Do not invent objections to justify the section.

### 3.9 Features

- Featured capability plus supporting collection.
- Anchored split demonstration.
- Editorial offset with product evidence.
- Content field for several interconnected capabilities.
- Sequence rail when capabilities form a workflow.
- Comparison view when the important story is a changed state or trade-off.

Avoid default equal cards and decorative product windows.

### 3.10 Footer

- Compact utility row for a small site.
- Grouped directory with product, company, resources, and legal destinations.
- Large destination index with one brand or contact statement.
- Media-backed close when imagery is part of the brand and links remain legible.
- Conversion-aware footer with a real newsletter or contact action separated from navigation.

Do not turn the footer into an unrelated final campaign.

### 3.11 Hero

- Full-bleed media with edge-anchored copy.
- Unequal split between promise and demonstration.
- Focused copy with a lower product or work preview.
- Copy over a controlled visual field.
- Editorial collage around a stable message core.
- Large top visual followed immediately by its explanation.
- Product stage with one interactive or cinematic proof object.
- Quiet typographic hero when the message and brand voice are sufficient.

Keep the page identity, promise, and primary route visible without overloading the first viewport with proof strips, feature lists, pricing teasers, and several CTA intents.

### 3.12 How It Works

- Compact ordered rail with one explanation per meaningful step.
- Sticky media sequence where one demonstration changes with the current chapter.
- Before, action, result narrative.
- Continuous workflow diagram with accessible text equivalents.
- Interactive walkthrough when the visitor benefits from controlling the sequence.

This section may reuse a Features or Showcase grammar. Use the label only when order or mechanism is the real question.

### 3.13 Navigation

Use the systems in [Navigation systems](#4-navigation-systems). Choose from the information architecture and visitor tasks, not visual novelty.

### 3.14 Newsletter

- Inline signup attached to relevant editorial content.
- Focused signup stack with a specific publishing promise.
- Preview-led split showing the real issue, cadence, or topics.
- Footer-integrated signup when it remains visible and distinct from link groups.

Do not imply frequency, audience size, or benefits that are not known.

### 3.15 Pricing

- Simple offer and action for one plan.
- Paired field for two meaningful choices.
- Plan collection when tiers serve distinct audiences.
- Criteria matrix when comparison detail drives the decision.
- Configurator or estimator when pricing truly depends on variables.

Do not arbitrarily highlight the middle plan or fabricate savings.

### 3.16 Showcase

- One case spotlight with problem, work, and result.
- Featured work plus collection.
- Editorial project index.
- Immersive full-bleed chapter sequence.
- Interactive media stage with a supporting project list.
- Content field for varied artifacts that share one story.

Avoid treating every screenshot as an equal card. Make project selection and progression clear.

### 3.17 Social Proof

- Case evidence stream.
- Source-linked quote with surrounding context.
- Client or partner index when identities are verified.
- Outcome narrative combining work and attributable results.
- Community or press collection when those sources are real and relevant.

Do not add a logo wall, quote carousel, or metric band by reflex.

### 3.18 Stats

- Annotated metric narrative explaining what each value means.
- One dominant metric with supporting evidence.
- Trend or change view when time is important.
- Compact evidence row inside a relevant section.
- Source-led data story with methodology or qualification.

Omit unknown numbers. Do not use decorative statistics.

### 3.19 Store

- Featured product plus collection.
- Category-led shelf.
- Editorial product story with contextual purchase action.
- Product grid with useful filters and consistent media.
- Curated collection with varied scale and one clear focus.
- Campaign stage leading into browsable products.

Preserve product information, price clarity, stock truth, and keyboard/touch shopping paths.

## 4. Navigation systems

### 4.1 Simple inline navigation

Place the brand at one edge, primary destinations in a stable central or adjacent group, and account or conversion actions at the other edge. Use when the destination set is small and understandable.

Keep desktop labels on one line. Do not hide ordinary destinations behind an elaborate interaction.

### 4.2 Announcement plus inline navigation

Place a short, dismissible or clearly linked announcement above the primary navigation. Use only for timely information that deserves repeated visibility. The announcement must not become permanent decorative copy.

### 4.3 Centered navigation island

Use a compact central navigation group while brand and utilities remain on the page edges. This works when the hierarchy is shallow and the island's visual treatment belongs to the brand. Keep hover, active, and expanded states stable without shifting the surrounding header.

### 4.4 Multi-column mega menu

Open a wide panel that groups many destinations by user task or product area. A category may include a short explanation or icon when it improves recognition.

Use when the information architecture genuinely exceeds an ordinary menu. Support click and keyboard opening, arrow or tab navigation, focus management, escape, outside dismissal, and a mobile alternative. Do not require hover alone.

### 4.5 Contextual preview mega panel

Pair navigation groups with selected previews such as locations, products, work, or resources. Change the preview only when it helps visitors choose. Keep the menu usable if media fails and prevent preview motion from stealing focus.

### 4.6 Full-screen menu takeover

Use the viewport as a destination index, optionally pairing large category links with featured stories, work, or contact paths. This suits brand-led sites with a compact primary trigger and a meaningful second layer.

Keep the close action persistent, lock and restore scroll correctly, trap and restore focus, expose current location, and avoid turning the takeover into a second homepage.

### 4.7 Compact vertical overlay

Open a narrow vertical panel or floating drawer over retained page context. Use when the menu itself is a strong branded object or when a compact set of destinations benefits from a focused reading path.

Ensure the panel is large enough for touch, does not obscure its own dismissal, and becomes a stable sheet or full-screen menu on narrow viewports.

### 4.8 Local or contextual subnavigation

Expand a panel immediately below the header for destinations within the current product, service, or location family. Keep the global navigation visible and make the relationship between trigger and panel explicit.

### Mobile navigation

Choose a sheet, full-screen index, accordion directory, or simple list based on depth. Do not squeeze the desktop mega menu into a narrow viewport. Preserve account and primary action access, current state, escape/back behavior, focus, scroll ownership, and sufficiently large targets.

## 5. Responsive transformation

### Preserve the content hierarchy

Keep the same promise, evidence, and action priority while allowing zones to reorder. Do not preserve desktop geometry at the expense of mobile comprehension.

### Transform by family

- **Full-bleed stage:** choose a new crop and safe zone; move copy outside the image when contrast cannot remain reliable.
- **Anchored split:** stack by reading priority, not automatically left then right. Allow edge-to-edge media while copy retains readable gutters.
- **Focused stack:** maintain measure and action clarity; avoid excessive vertical centering that pushes key controls below the fold.
- **Content field:** reduce object count, establish one dominant object, or turn supporting objects into a controlled horizontal sequence.
- **Editorial offset:** preserve intentional contrast through scale and order rather than tiny offsets.
- **Collection:** change spans and featured status; do not compress three desktop columns into unreadable cards.
- **Sequence rail:** use a vertical flow or user-controlled media state. Avoid mandatory scroll tricks.
- **Paired field:** stack with shared criteria close to both options or allow a labeled toggle when simultaneous comparison is unnecessary.
- **Matrix:** transform by criterion or contain horizontal scrolling locally with clear axis labels.
- **Utility directory:** group destinations in accordions or stable lists while keeping legal and primary actions reachable.

### Test intermediate widths

Inspect content-driven breakpoints, long labels, text zoom, dynamic viewport height, image crop, form controls, menus, and sections with absolute or sticky positioning. Recompose before reducing type or target size.

## 6. Variation and quality gate

### Useful variation levers

Change several of these together when adapting a family:

- dominant content type;
- grid anchor and alignment;
- zone ratio and negative-space owner;
- headline scale and measure;
- media crop, mask, and behavior;
- item density and featured-item logic;
- border, depth, and surface treatment from the brand system;
- action placement and commitment level;
- interaction model;
- mobile order and transformation;
- motion role and intensity.

Changing only color, radius, or image content does not create a meaningful variation.

### Final questions

Before accepting a layout, ask:

1. Does the composition make the section's job easier to understand?
2. Is the hierarchy clear without decorative labels?
3. Does every visual object have a content or compositional role?
4. Does this section change the page's rhythm without feeling unrelated?
5. Is the adaptation recognizably part of the current brand rather than the reference?
6. Does mobile preserve the argument rather than merely stack boxes?
7. Can keyboard, touch, zoom, and reduced-motion users reach the same information and actions?
8. Would removing an effect, card, or section improve the result?
